# Content Strategy

## Purpose

Every piece of published content must support Aston Cyber's mission.

---

# Content Pillars

## Research

Original investigations.

Evidence-based findings.

White papers.

Technical reports.

---

## Engineering

Open-source software.

Reference implementations.

Security tooling.

Architecture.

Automation.

Completed or substantially complete work is published as a Project.

---

## Education

Guides.

Books.

Diagrams.

---

## Commentary

Thoughtful analysis supported by evidence.

Industry observations.

Emerging technology.

---

## Labs

Open-source security tools.

Utilities and scripts.

Proofs-of-concept.

Detection content.

Early experiments and creative security ventures.

Prototype work.

Ideas in progress.

Research journals.

Labs must identify maturity and preliminary or experimental status clearly.

---

# Collection Boundaries

- Research publishes formal investigations and technical reports.
- Projects publishes completed or substantially complete cybersecurity work.
- Labs publishes tooling, experiments, proof-of-concepts, detection content,
  and other creative security ventures.
- Shared components and metadata must not collapse these distinct editorial
  concepts.
- Writing and Speaking belong to the future personal website at
  `matt.astoncyber.com`, not the Aston Cyber content roadmap.

---

# Publishing Standard

Every publication MUST answer:

1. What problem exists?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What evidence supports the conclusions?
4. What was learned?
5. What should others do next?

If these questions cannot be answered, the work is not ready to publish.

---

# Editorial Requirements

- Publish to educate first.
- Avoid sensationalism.
- Cite evidence whenever practical.
- Prefer diagrams over marketing graphics.
- Favor clarity over cleverness.
- Maintain a calm, research-oriented tone.
- Every article should leave the reader more capable than before.
